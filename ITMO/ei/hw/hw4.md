1. Susan David and Christina Congleton define emotional agility as an ability to manage our thoughts and feelings in a mindful, productive and values-driven way. It means that we dont suppress negative emotions, but also dont let them control our actions. 
2. There are four main practices: recognize your patterns, label your thoughts and emotions, accept them, and act according to your values. 
3. A person can recognize that they are “on the hook” when their thoughts become rigid and repetitive, like the same thing is playing again and again in their head. Another sign is when the situation feels very familiar, like you already had the same thoughts and reactions in the past. Basically, first you need to notice that you are stuck in the same pattern.
